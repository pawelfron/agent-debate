# Notes of the Greyfell flood inquiry, October 1912

Margaret Adey, a barrister, opened the inquiry. She is the daughter of Councillor Edmund Adey, who died in 1909. She is not a member of the borough council, and she did not sit as a councillor.

A resurvey in September 1911 measured the crest at 18.2 metres above the streambed at the upper intake. The 1897 report had recommended 18.5 metres. The engineer who gave evidence put the difference down to settlement, not to a revised design. No contract drawing shows a crest of sixty feet.

A quarryman swore that a cart of Pethwick stone "went into the core" in August 1898. The yard book of 11 August 1898, signed in by Jonas Pell, shows one Pethwick load, with a note in Helena Voss's hand that it was a sample and not for the dam. The inquiry finds that this particular load was not placed in the core. The commissioners did not open the core, and they record that limit in the same paragraph.

The spillway carries a mark from the overflow of May 1900 and a higher mark from December 1911. During the 1911 flood, Low Greyfell drew none of the filtered town water.
