# Calderbridge Gazette, 18 May 1900

The Greyfell reservoir overflowed for the first time yesterday, after rain on the moor. Councillor Edmund Adey, speaking at the spillway, said the town weir had already been drawing water since March. He put the cost at "about fifty thousand pounds". He thanked "the contractor, Pell", and he thanked the borough surveyor, Owen Hale, "whose shed has kept the mud out of the main".

A separate paragraph says that Jonas Pell, called young Pell, broke a wrist on the pitching in April 1899. He afterwards kept the Holne tally and the yard book. The paragraph does not say that he signed the articles or that he is the principal of M. Pell & Sons.

The paper prints Adey's "nearly sixty feet of earth" as a sentence from his speech. The correspondent took no measurement. The paper does not mention a filtration house, a sand bed, or Miriam Pell.
