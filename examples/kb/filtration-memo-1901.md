# Memo, borough surveyor to the town clerk, 4 November 1901

Owen Hale writes that the filtration house beside the town weir was completed on 22 October 1901. It is brick, with three sand beds. He designed it in the borough office after Helena Voss declined to put it on her drawings. It was never part of the Pell contract. The council paid £6,140 for it from the town works account.

From 22 October 1901, every gallon sent into the town main has passed those sand beds. Water taken at the weir before that day, including the draw that Councillor Adey dated from March 1900, passed only the gravel screen at the upper intake.

Low Greyfell is above the weir and is not connected to this main. The timber shed has been taken down. It screened the pipe trench. It was not a filter, and it was not the dam.
