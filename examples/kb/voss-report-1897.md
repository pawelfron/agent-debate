# Report on the upper intake of Greyfell Beck

Helena Voss, for Voss & Keene, 3 April 1897, to the promoters of the Calderbridge Waterworks Company.

The streambed at the upper intake is rock under thin gravel. An earth dam with a clay core, 18.5 metres from streambed to crest, would hold about 1.2 million cubic metres. That covers a dry August for the town and still leaves a margin for Low Greyfell. The hamlet lies above the town weir, so the town main cannot serve it.

Pitching should come from Holne quarry. Pethwick stone is cheaper and looks alike when wet, but it flakes after frost. Voss will not accept it in the core or the pitching. A labelled sample may sit in the yard if it is marked not for the dam.

With Holne stone, the dam, the spillway, and the pipe to the town weir come to £41,200. The same works in Pethwick stone would be £36,800, and she would refuse to sign them. Neither sum includes a filtration house. She has not designed one. The drawn pipe stops at the weir.

If a contractor is on the ground before August 1897, she would expect water at the weir in the summer of 1899, provided Holne quarry stays open. That expectation is not a term of any contract.
